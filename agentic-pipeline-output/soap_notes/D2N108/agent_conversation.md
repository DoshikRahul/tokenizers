**Preliminary diagnosis:**

Diabetic foot ulcer with secondary infection
Cellulitis of the right foot

**Diagnostic discussion:**

Gregory presents with a non-healing foot ulcer on the top of the right foot near the fifth toe, approximately two months in duration, with redness, swelling, drainage of fluid, and intermittent throbbing. His history of diabetes mellitus with an A1C of ~10 indicates poor glycemic control, which is a significant risk factor for diabetic foot ulcers and delayed healing. The presence of redness, swelling, and drainage suggests a secondary bacterial infection, likely cellulitis. The absence of fever or systemic symptoms suggests a localized infection rather than a more severe systemic condition. Differential diagnoses include diabetic foot ulcer with secondary infection (most likely given the history of diabetes and ulcer characteristics), cellulitis (supported by the presence of redness, swelling, and drainage), and osteomyelitis (less likely without evidence of bone involvement or systemic symptoms). The lack of peripheral neuropathy or vascular issues reported by the patient may suggest that the ulcer is not due to neuroischemic changes, but further evaluation is needed to confirm this. The absence of recent laboratory or imaging results limits the ability to fully assess the extent of infection or underlying vascular status.

**Proposed plan:**

1. Further evaluation: Obtain wound culture and sensitivity to guide antibiotic selection. Consider imaging (e.g., X-ray or MRI) to rule out osteomyelitis. Perform vascular assessment (e.g., ankle-brachial index, Doppler studies) to evaluate for peripheral arterial disease. 2. Medication: Initiate broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., oral cephalosporin or clindamycin) pending culture results. Continue prescribed antibiotics once available. Monitor for adverse effects and ensure adherence. 3. Non-drug management and education: Educate on proper wound care, including regular cleaning and dressing changes. Emphasize strict glycemic control (target A1C <7%) and regular monitoring. Recommend a low-sodium diet and weight management if applicable. Advise on avoiding pressure on the ulcer and using appropriate footwear. Educate on red flags requiring emergency care, such as worsening pain, fever, or spreading redness. 4. Follow-up: Schedule a follow-up in one week to reassess the ulcer, review culture results, and adjust treatment as needed. Ensure adherence to prescribed medications and wound care. Coordinate with diabetes and podiatry specialists for ongoing management.
