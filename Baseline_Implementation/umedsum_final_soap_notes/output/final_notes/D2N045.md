# D2N045 - uMedSum final note

## Subjective
Chief Complaint: Right knee pain
History of Present Illness (HPI): The patient, Abigail, presents with a chief complaint of right knee pain, which started a few months ago.
The pain is described as a dull ache, especially on the outside of the right knee, worsened by running and being on feet all day.
The patient reports that ice and ibuprofen provide temporary relief.

## Objective
Physical Examination & Vitals: The patient's physical examination revealed a small amount of effusion (fluid under the knee) with no redness or swelling.
There was no pain or tenderness on movement, and no signs of instability.
Vital signs were not mentioned.
Diagnostic Results & Labs: X-rays taken showed normal joint spaces and no sign of fracture or arthritis.

## Assessment and Plan (Stage 1, unchanged)
* **Diagnoses and Clinical Reasoning:** Based on the patient's symptoms and physical examination findings, the diagnosis is likely related to the effusion in the right knee. Further evaluation is needed to determine the underlying cause of the effusion.
* **Treatment Plan, Medications, and Follow-up:** The patient will undergo further evaluation, including blood tests and potentially imaging studies, to determine the underlying cause of the effusion. The patient will be advised to continue using ice and ibuprofen as needed for pain relief. Follow-up instructions will be provided once the diagnosis is confirmed and a treatment plan is established.
