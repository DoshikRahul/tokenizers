Abstract
A clinical intake system produces one record
that two people read: the doctor signs a SOAP
note, and the patient receives a plain-language
summary. Current LLM systems collect the
evidence first and verify the record afterwards.
We argue that this separation causes two fail-
ures. Unrecoverable gaps: verification finds
a missing or unsupported fact only after the
patient, the one source who could supply it,
has left the conversation, so the fact can only
be deleted or guessed. A fact–inference cate-
gory error: faithfulness filters test every sen-
tence for entailment, but diagnoses and plans
are inferences that no dialogue turn entails, so
correct reasoning is deleted. We make both fail-
ures measurable with known answers (withheld
facts, planted errors, valid assessments) and a
checkpoint-branching protocol in which every
compared strategy continues from the identi-
cal intake dialogue. We then test two minimal
fixes, verify-then-ask and section-aware verifi-
cation; all other components are held fixed and
evaluated only as supporting parts.
1 Problem and Motivation
Problem statement. LLM agents can take a pa-
tient history and write a clinical note, but the note
may contain facts the patient never stated and omit
facts the patient would have reported if asked. In-
teractive intake systems such as Aegle (Chen et al.,
2026) reduce omissions by asking better questions,
but do not check individual statements in the note.
Post-hoc filters such as uMedSum (Nagar et al.,
2025) check atomic facts, but only after the source
is fixed, and with one entailment test for every sen-
tence. Chaining the two leaves a structural gap:
when the filter finds that a mandatory fact, such
as an allergy, is missing or unsupported, it can
only delete the statement or paste in similar text,
because the patient can no longer be asked. The
filter also cannot tell a reported fact (“chest pain
for two hours”) from a clinical inference (“likely
stable angina”); the inference is never entailed by
the dialogue, so a correct diagnosis is removed.
Both errors reach both readers: the doctor signs an
incomplete or over-pruned note, and the patient’s
summary inherits it.
Why it matters. The first-visit note anchors later
decisions, so a dropped allergy or a deleted correct
assessment propagates through care. The doctor’s
review effort depends on how much of the note
must be re-checked, and the patient’s summary
carries the clinic’s authority even when it is wrong.
Domain and target users. Healthcare, restricted
to adult outpatient first-visit intake and the SOAP
Initial Progress Note. The patient answers plain-
language questions and, after sign-off, receives a
lay summary; red-flag symptoms trigger an imme-
diate alert. The doctor reviews flagged items and
signs a note in which every statement links to its
evidence.
What is missing in existing approaches. Intake
agents (Chen et al., 2026) are evaluated mainly with
rubric scores, not statement-level checks. Atomic-
fact and NLI-based checking (Min et al., 2023;
Laban et al., 2022; Nagar et al., 2025) and self-
refinement (Madaan et al., 2023) assume the source
is complete and fixed, and treat every sentence as
extractive. Clarifying-question research (Alianne-
jadi et al., 2019) shows that asking resolves uncer-
tainty, but it is not triggered by claim-level verifica-
tion. No prior work (i) uses a verification result to
ask the source while it is still available, or (ii) sep-
arates facts from inferences when deciding what
counts as supported.
2 Research Questions and Hypotheses
RQ1 (unrecoverable gaps). At a matched budget
of patient turns, does asking a targeted question
when verification finds a gap recover more missing

facts, without lowering faithfulness, than deleting,
guessing, or asking more untargeted questions?
RQ2 (category error). With the same verifier
model, does checking Assessment/Plan (A/P) state-
ments by their evidence chain instead of by en-
tailment keep more correct reasoning while still
rejecting unsupported diagnoses and plans?
• H1a: Verify-then-ask recovers more withheld
facts than delete-only and than post-hoc filtering.
• H1b: It recovers more than the same number
of untargeted extra turns, so targeting drives the
gain.
• H1c: Its grounding rate is at least that of post-hoc
filtering, while guessing lowers grounding.
• H2: Uniform entailment rejects many valid A/P
statements even with our verifier; the evidence-
chain rule rejects fewer while keeping recall on
planted unsupported A/P statements within 5
points.
• H3 (secondary): Patient summaries built from
the verified record contain fewer unsupported
statements at similar reading level.
3 Capabilities: Core vs. Supporting
Two capabilities answer the research questions.
The others are implementation choices; each has
exactly one check, and we claim nothing beyond it
(Table 1). Multi-agent intake does not test either re-
search question—it only produces the dialogue that
verification acts on—so it is held fixed for every
comparison and varied once only, to test whether
the RQ1 gain depends on intake quality.
4 Proposed Approach
Figure 1 shows one encounter. Notation: dialogue
turns D = (d1, . . . , dT ); SOAP features F = {fi},
each linked to its source; atomic claims C = {cj}
from the draft note.
Fixed intake. We reuse Aegle’s design: an or-
chestrator activates up to three specialists, who
propose questions in parallel without seeing each
other, and an aggregator writes the state before ask-
ing. Intake stops when mandatory fields are filled,
or three turns before the cap. This point is the
checkpoint.
Verify-then-ask (RQ1). The draft is split into
atomic claims, and each claim is checked against
evidence from its own section: Subjective claims
against the top-k retrieved patient turns, Objective
claims against the encounter’s measurement record
(vitals and exam values, which a patient cannot
report). A verifier labels each claim supported,
contradicted or unsupported, and fixed controller
rules act: supported → keep; contradicted with the
correct value in a cited turn → correct from that
turn and re-verify; an unsupported or missing Sub-
jective fact about a symptom, medication, allergy,
duration or mandatory field → ask one targeted
question; an Objective gap → flag for the doctor;
anything else → delete. The answer is re-verified,
and the claim is kept with the new turn as evidence,
deleted if denied, or marked “patient unsure”. At
most three questions are asked. Rules, not the LLM,
choose the action, so every action is reproducible
and logged with its reason.
Section-aware verification (RQ2). After the
freeze, specialists write the Assessment and Plan,
and each statement must cite the features it rests
on. Because A/P statements are inferences, the
verifier does not test entailment; it judges whether
the cited features, together with a retrieved refer-
ence passage when one is found, justify the state-
ment (chain_ok / chain_broken). A broken chain
is flagged for the doctor rather than deleted, since
removing a diagnosis is a clinical decision.
Two readers. The doctor receives the note with
each statement linked to its evidence, plus the flags.
After sign-off (simulated in experiments), a patient
summary is generated from the signed note, exclud-
ing any item still flagged; each summary sentence
is verified against the signed note before release.
Supporting components. Verifier: one model
for every check, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct with QLoRA
(Dettmers et al., 2023) (rank 16), trained on
∼6k claim–evidence pairs from MTS-Dialog
(Ben Abacha et al., 2023) and ACI-Bench train
with six perturbation types; S/O and A/P use differ-
ent prompts and label sets. Retrieval: BM25 and
dense embeddings fused by reciprocal rank (Cor-
mack et al., 2009); k ∈ {1, 3, 5} is tuned on dev for
evidence recall. Reference passages: MedlinePlus
topic pages (public domain) for the test complaints,
chunked by section; these are consumer-level ref-
erences, not clinical practice guidelines. Tools:
guards block writes after the freeze, diagnosis or
treatment wording to the patient before sign-off, fi-
nalising with an unresolved red flag, and release be-
fore sign-off. All agents use Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.

5 Evaluation Plan
Data and known answers. All data are public
or synthetic; no real patient takes part. E1 (40 en-
counters + 10 dev): ACI-Bench (Yim et al., 2023)
test1 (dev: valid). An LLM simulated patient an-
swers only from a hidden case file of atomic facts,
checked by two team members. In each case two
mandatory Subjective facts are withheld: revealed
only to a direct question. Objective facts form the
measurement record; eight cases contain a planted
red flag. E2 (120 notes): drafts from E1 reference
notes, 90 with one planted error (fabricated symp-
tom, negation flip, medication or dose, laterality or
duration, unsupported diagnosis, plan without evi-
dence; 15 each) and 30 unchanged; the reference
A/P statements form the valid set.
Checkpoint branching and baselines. Intake
runs once per case and seed and is saved at the
checkpoint. Every RQ1 condition continues from
that saved state with the same note writer and at
most three more patient turns, so comparisons are
paired on identical dialogues. Every RQ2 condition
receives the same frozen A/P statements and is
scored on its reject decision (flag or delete), so our
rule cannot win simply by never deleting. Table 2
lists all conditions.
Metrics. RQ1: withheld-fact recovery (withheld
facts present and supported in the final note);
atomic and critical fact recall (allergies, medica-
tions, red-flag symptoms); S/O grounding rate; hal-
lucination on gaps; patient turns. Diagnostics to
explain outcomes: gap-detection precision/recall,
gaps resolved per question, redundant-question
rate. RQ2: valid-claim retention (valid A/P state-
ments not rejected) and unsupported-A/P rejec-
tion precision/recall; per-step verdict accuracy. Pa-
tient reader: unsupported statements per summary,
recall of next steps and warning signs, Flesch–
Kincaid grade. Supporting: verifier macro-F1 per
error type, evidence recall@k, citation precision,

6 Expected Novelty
1. A named, measured failure of the intake-then-
verify design: verification that comes too late
to fix a gap, and entailment as the wrong test for
clinical inferences, shown with known answers
rather than rubric scores.
2. A reusable evaluation protocol: checkpoint
branching with withheld facts compares any ver-
ification strategy on identical dialogues.
3. Evidence that minimal fixes suffice:two small,
explainable changes, each tested against a con-
trol that removes only that change.
7 Compute Plan
GTX 1080 Ti GPUs (compute capability 6.1)
are not supported by vLLM’s standard builds, so
the 7B backbone and the simulated patient run
through OpenRouter (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, listed
at US$0.10/$0.20 per million input/output tokens);
ACI-Bench is public, so this is permitted. The clus-
ter runs verifier training and inference, DeBERTa
NLI and retrieval. Kaggle and Colab are backups.
