# D2N168 — signed SOAP note

## Subjective
* Patient is a 28-year-old female who presented with a recent episode of hypertension, feeling light-headed, and almost falling. She has been taking lisinopril for hypertension.
* The patient reports that her boyfriend was present when she experienced the episode.
* She reports no chest pain, palpitations, or visual disturbances during the episode.
* The patient reports that her boyfriend discovered her and she was taken directly to the emergency department.
* The patient reports that the information about undergoing imaging studies is not available to them.
* The patient reports that information regarding plasma aldosterone-to-renin ratio, cortisol levels, or adrenal imaging is not available.
* The patient reports that the results of the most recent physical examination are unknown.
* The patient reports that the blood pressure reading from the ER was not recorded, but the patient was informed that it was approximately 200 mmHg.
* The patient reports that the headache was intense, but it resolved after arrival at the emergency room.
* She has a history of high blood pressure, which is usually triggered by traveling for work and not eating well.
* She has no family history of hypertension, migraines, or neurological conditions.
* She has not had similar episodes before, but her blood pressure has been running high once a month or so.

## Objective
* The patient reports having a slightly leaky heart valve, as shown on an echocardiogram performed a few months ago.
* The patient's cardiovascular examination revealed a slight murmur.
* The patient exhibited trace pitting edema in their lower extremities.

## Assessment
* The patient has a history of hypertension, which is currently uncontrolled.
* She has experienced an episode of light-headedness and almost falling, which may be related to her hypertension.
* The patient has a slightly leaky heart valve, but no other structural heart disease was found.

## Plan
* The patient will continue taking lisinopril as prescribed.
* The doctor will consider further evaluation for secondary causes of hypertension, including endocrine evaluations and imaging studies.
* The patient will be advised to monitor her blood pressure regularly and to report any further episodes or changes in her condition.
